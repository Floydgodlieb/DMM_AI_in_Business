# The AI-literacy duty (AI Act Article 4)

The knowledge gap is not only a business problem: a firm that uses AI has a legal duty to close it for the people who use the tools.

## The duty

- A business that provides or uses AI systems must take measures to build AI literacy among the people who run or use them [W4-003]; the Dutch DPA guidance, as reported, says the same for staff and contractors [W4-040].
- It has applied since 2 February 2025; supervision and enforcement apply from 3 August 2026 [W4-006].
- National market surveillance authorities enforce it, not the AI Office [W4-007].
- It does not require measuring employees' AI knowledge [W4-005].
- The Commission and Member States must support SMEs in meeting it [W4-004].

## How to meet it

- Management must put AI literacy on the agenda, budget for it and support it [W4-038], top-down as well as bottom-up [W4-039].
- Make sure there is awareness and training on risks and opportunities [W4-009], and ask the supplier what training implementation needs [W4-010].
- The Dutch government framework states the same requirement for its own bodies [W4-008].

## Why it matters in daily use

- Data typed into an AI tool may not be protected [W4-020], and AI-made text may be wrong [W4-021].

## Gaps

- No claim says which Dutch authority supervises Article 4 for private firms.
- No claim gives a cost or time for meeting the duty in a firm of the reader's size.
