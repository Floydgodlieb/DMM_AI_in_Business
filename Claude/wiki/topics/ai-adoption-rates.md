# AI adoption rates

A firm of the reader's size is less likely than average to use AI, even though trade is the sector with the most AI users. Every source that compares sizes finds small firms behind large ones; the levels differ by definition, population and year.

## The picture

### Netherlands
- In 2024, 22.7% of Dutch firms with 10 or more staff used at least one AI technology, almost 9 points up on 2023 [W2-001].
- Use rises with size, from 17.8% at 10-19 staff to 59.2% at 500+ staff [W2-002].
- Small firms (10-49 staff) are under-represented among AI users [W2-003]; trade holds the largest share of AI-using firms [W2-004].
- CBS 2025 (provisional): one in six businesses uses AI, double two years earlier [W4-155]; firms with 50 to 250 staff rose from 20 percent to 45 percent [W4-156]; 35 percent of users apply it to marketing or sales [W4-157]; three quarters neither use nor have considered it [W4-158].
- Among Dutch workers (a survey of people, not firms), 43 percent use AI at work [W4-139].

### EU and euro area
- Eurostat 2025: 20.0% of EU firms with 10+ staff used AI, up from 13.5% [W4-118]; Denmark highest, Romania lowest [W4-119]; the survey excludes smaller firms [W4-121].
- ECB survey (firm self-reports): 38% of euro area firms at an advanced stage [W4-114], around 45% of large firms and 56% of young firms [W4-115]; large, listed and young firms adopt more than small, unlisted, established ones [W4-116].
- Euro area employees adopting AI: 26 per cent in 2024, 40 per cent in 2025 [W4-111].
- In 2025, 37% of EU firms deployed generative AI, about level with US firms [W4-036].
- Across Europe the largest firms adopt most, in data from before generative AI [W4-061].

### OECD, G7 and beyond
- OECD: large firms' AI use (40%) is more than three times small firms' (11.9%) [W4-179]; the share of firms rose from 5.6% to 14% in 2020 to 2024 [W4-180].
- AI use is consistently lower among SMEs than large firms in all G7 countries [W4-143] [W4-178]; in transportation, construction and hospitality it is in the single digits [W4-145].
- OECD SME survey, seven countries (self-reports): generative AI in 31% of SMEs [W4-173].
- US survey of adults: nearly 40 percent use generative AI; 23 percent of employed respondents used it for work in the past week [W4-132] [W4-133].

## Contradictions and tensions

> [!contradiction] W2-001 vs W4-155: 22.7% of Dutch firms in 2024 vs one in six in 2025
> An apparent fall that may not be one, because the 2025 claim gives no size class: CBS counts firms with 10+ staff using any AI technology in 2024 [W2-001]; the 2025 CBS release is provisional [W4-155]. Check definitions and population before quoting a trend.

> [!contradiction] W4-118 vs W4-114 vs W4-173: 20.0% (Eurostat), 38% (ECB), 31% (OECD) of firms use AI
> Different measures: Eurostat counts firms with 10+ staff using AI technologies, independently collected [W4-118]; the ECB figure is firms at an advanced stage by self-report [W4-114]; the OECD figure is generative AI in SMEs of up to 249 staff in seven non-Dutch countries, self-reported [W4-173] [W4-172].

> [!contradiction] W4-156 vs W2-002: firms with 50 to 250 staff at 45 percent vs the 2024 gradient
> The newer CBS size band is 50 to 250 staff in 2025 (provisional) [W4-156]; the 2024 CBS bands run up to 500+ [W2-002]. The reader's firm (10 to 50 staff) is covered by neither band exactly.

> [!contradiction] W2-001 vs W4-036: 22.7% of Dutch firms used AI, 37% of EU firms deploy generative AI
> Different measures: CBS counts firms with 10+ staff using any AI technology in 2024 [W2-001]; the EIB counts all firms deploying generative AI in 2025 [W4-036]. Neither is the reader's size class alone.

> [!contradiction] W4-061 vs W2-002: the European study's adoption data is from 2020, before generative AI
> Its size gradient matches CBS, but its levels cannot be compared with 2024-2025 figures [W4-059].

## Gaps

- No claim gives AI use for Dutch trade firms of the reader's size specifically.
- No claim covers used-vehicle or machinery traders at all ([[S-017]] was discarded).
- Eurostat's Netherlands figure is not in the extract [W4-119].
