# Sanctions and export screening

For a trader selling outside the EU, the export-document step carries a legal duty that screening tools can support.

## The duty

- Some exports to some countries are banned or need a permit [W2-005].
- The EU bans exports to Russia of dual-use goods and advanced technology [W2-006] and restricts transport equipment and aviation, space and maritime goods [W2-007].
- The legal basis is Regulation (EU) No 833/2014 [W2-008].

## Tools

- Denied-party screening checks trading partners against government watchlists [W2-026].
- One vendor claims its AI cuts false positives by 60%, a figure it reports itself [W2-027].

## The customs data hub

- The EU adopted a major customs reform on 16 September 2026 [W4-079]; the EU Customs Data Hub becomes the single digital interface for customs operations [W4-081]. Using it is mandatory for e-commerce from 1 July 2028 and for all traders from 1 March 2034 [W4-080]. Reliable traders may self-release goods [W4-082].
- Taken together: the sanctions duty sits on the same export-document step that will move to the data hub; no claim says how screening and the hub will connect. See [[ai-in-trade-and-customs]].

## Gaps

- No claim says whether used commercial vehicles or machinery fall under the restricted transport categories.
- No claim on what screening costs a small trader.
