# Week 3 course brief — AIBS handbook page

Source: `_skill_material/ai-in-business-source/site/week-03.html` (the published course site — the
legitimate skill-input scope per `SKILL_MATERIAL_SCOPE.md`). Condensed to the AIBS handbook-page
assignment and hand-in terms; the AEL knowledge-architecture assignment, the solo source-choosing
exercise, and the interview guide are out of scope for this pipeline (see `CLAUDE.md`).

## Theme

**Trust and AI safety.** "For two weeks you have asked what AI can do for an SME. This week the
owner asks something harder: can I trust it with my data, with its answers and with my people?
And can I trust the team that tells me so?"

## The assignment, verbatim (callout "AIBS — the handbook page: trust and AI safety")

> One page of your team's handbook, written for the SME you named in your research proposal. It
> answers the question that owner would ask: *can I trust this — with my data, with its answers,
> and with my people?* Take the three parts in turn. What leaves the building when the firm uses
> AI, and could it run the model itself? When may an AI tool act on the firm's behalf, and when
> only advise? And what happens when staff use AI and do not say so?
>
> It follows the chapter template and meets the four criteria on it. Every claim carries an
> appraised source: who says it, and one line on why they are worth believing. Say on the page
> what your platform drafted and what your team checked. A reader who cannot tell will trust the
> whole page less. It ends in something the owner can do.
>
> Scope and depth are confirmed in the session. *(this remains a `.tpl` placeholder on the course
> site as of 2026-09-22 — not yet published as a fixed word budget; flagged as an open question
> for Floyd rather than guessed at, see `01_requirements/checklist.md`)*
>
> **The bar:** an owner reading it knows what to hand to AI, what to keep in the building, and
> what to ask their staff.

## The four floor criteria (from the chapter template, `handbook.html#about`)

Every chapter is measured against: **clarity** for a non-technical reader, **accuracy** against
appraised sources, **responsible-AI coverage**, and **usefulness to an SME owner**.

## The chapter template's disclaimer (verbatim, `handbook.html#about`)

> This handbook offers inspiration and starting points, not commercial advice or guarantees.
> Every chapter carries this disclaimer. Decisions about a firm remain the firm's own.

## Hand-in terms

- **What:** one published handbook page per team, alongside the AEL knowledge architecture
  (out of scope here) and each person's portfolio entry (never AI-drafted, out of scope here).
- **Where/when:** due by the start of the next session, **Monday 28 September 2026**. Handed in
  by being published on the shared course site (a live page, not a repo link or a Google Doc).
- **The gate:** the page goes to the team's partner team for review. Not scored directly — it is
  evidence for the final interview and feeds the portfolio ("what did the gates ask, and what
  did you change"). Not this pipeline's own internal gate (see `CLAUDE.md`, "two kinds of gate").
- **AI-use disclosure:** "Say on the page what your platform drafted and what your team checked."
  This is a course requirement, restated as global rule 10 in `CLAUDE.md`.
