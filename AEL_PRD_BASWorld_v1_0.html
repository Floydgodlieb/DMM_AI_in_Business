1. Problem and users

Primary users. Our own four-person research team.

Downstream readers. Commercial and operations management at BAS World, who read a published page to decide on a next AI step. Behind them stands the handbook's standing reader, a non-technical owner of a much smaller firm in the same trade.

The problem it solves. We publish one well-founded handbook page every week for six weeks, built from Dutch and English material plus one Dutch-language interview. Done by hand, the searching, appraising, translating and drafting will not fit inside a week, and the page that comes out cannot be traced back to what it was built from. Dutch material creates a second problem: whoever happens to read Dutch decides what gets researched.

2. What the platform lets a user do

#StepWhat the user doesWhat the platform does
1Gather sourcesStates the sub-question, adds any known sourcesSearches Dutch and English material, stores each hit with URL, publisher and date
2AppraiseReviews the score, overrides it when wrongScores credibility, relevance and recency in the source's own language, writes a reason per source
3Process the interviewUploads the recording, confirms consent is on fileTranscribes, anonymises by role, keeps the transcript inside the team environment
4DraftSets the angle, editsSummarises per sub-question, drafts against the chapter template, attaches a source reference to every claim
5CheckRuns the checklist, fixes what failsTests the draft against the six qualities in section 3, reports what it misses
6PublishApprovesCommits the reviewed page to the existing course wiki and site

Build order. Retrieval and the language path come first, not the checklist. Our stated pain is language and source handling, so a checklist that runs on pages we cannot yet produce buys us nothing. Order: (1) retrieval, appraisal, translation, (2) summarisation, (3) template drafting, (4) checklist.

3. Output qualities

Floor criteria from the chapter template. Clear to a non-technical reader. Accurate against appraised sources. Covers responsible AI. Useful to an owner.

A good page also

#Quality
1Attaches every recommendation to a named step in BAS World's sales or documentation chain, never to a category of tool.
2Keeps time-saving automation apart from automation that touches the customer relationship, and says which of the two applies.
3Names the rule in play per recommendation (AI Act, GDPR, sanctions screening) and what evidence compliance would need.
4Labels each figure company-reported or independently verifiable.
5States what a trader with 10 to 50 staff in the same business would need to run a smaller version of it.
6Ends on one concrete next step: an owner, a rough cost, and a way to tell inside three months whether it worked.

Cross-check. Identical to section 4 of the research proposal. Qualities 3 and 4 are the two the platform enforces mechanically: every claim carries a source reference, and company-reported figures get flagged when they enter the store. The rest rely on the reviewer.

Team constraint. Exactly one of us reads Dutch fluently. Three of those six fallbacks run through that one person, which is a single point of failure, and it is why the language path gets built early rather than late.

5. Handling Dutch source material

Dutch material (CBS, KvK, sector bodies, regional press, our own interview recording) carries things the English sources do not. The handbook itself is written in English.

Retrieval. Queries go out in both languages. Dutch queries get generated from the sub-question rather than translated from the English query, so that Dutch trade vocabulary such as tweedehands bedrijfswagens, exportdocumentatie and sanctielijst show up in the results.

Appraisal before translation. A source gets scored in the language it was written in. Score a machine translation and you score the translation: a hedge or qualifier that got flattened is what turns a weak source into a strong-looking one. Only sources that pass appraisal go on to be translated.

Translation. The original stays beside the translation. Figures, amounts, dates and quoted text get checked against the original before any claim built on them reaches a draft.

Why the tool does this and not a person. Language should not decide who on the team can research, and our one Dutch reader belongs in the reviewer seat rather than the bottleneck.

Where things run. CLI and source store run locally on team machines. Language and translation calls go to hosted models we already have access to. The published page lives in the course repository and site.

Decisions already taken. No model gets trained: this is existing models driven by prompts. Appraisal runs before translation, for the reason given in section 5.

8. Out of scope for now

  • Any interface beyond the CLI. Nobody outside the team uses this thing.
  • Publishing without human review. That review is the product's main control, not a delay.
  • Storing or processing the interview recording anywhere outside the environment we agreed on.
  • Output in more than one language. The handbook is English only.
  • Fine-tuning or training any model on BAS World material.
  • Anything built for a second partner company. One case, done properly.

9. Open questions and assumptions

What we are assuming

  • BAS World hosts one visit during weeks 2 to 4, and sends somebody from export documentation rather than sales alone.
  • Hosted language and translation services we already have access to will do the job, so no budget is needed.
  • The chapter template will not change much during the module.

What we still need answered

Open questionWhy it matters
Where may the Dutch recording be processed?If transcription has to stay inside the EU, or off third-party services altogether, phase 2 tooling changes. Settle this first: it blocks the build, and it is the concrete version of the vendor dependency argument the research proposal makes in the abstract.
Will we trust an automated appraisal score, or re-check every source by hand anyway?If the second, the platform saves nothing and phase 1 needs rethinking rather than extending.
How do we measure "ready to publish" without it collapsing into "the reviewer was tired"?Two countable proposals: share of claims that resolve to an appraised source (target 100 percent), and number of quality criteria failed at first review (target 1 or fewer). Both get trialled in week 2 before we commit.

The bar. A reader can tell what we are building, who it is for, and how we will know it works.
