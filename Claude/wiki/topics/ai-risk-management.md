# AI risk management

Two bodies of text say how to manage AI risk: a voluntary US framework and the binding AI Act. For a trader the binding part is small: literacy for everyone who uses AI, and more only if a system is high-risk.

## The voluntary framework (NIST, US)

- It is voluntary and fits organisations of all sizes and sectors [W4-098].
- Four functions: GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE [W4-099]; GOVERN runs through everything, the others apply to specific systems and lifecycle stages [W4-100].

## The binding rules (AI Act)

- Everyone who uses AI: build AI literacy among staff and others using AI on the firm's behalf [W4-091], with no guaranteed level per person [W4-092]. See [[ai-literacy-duty]].
- Users of high-risk systems: follow the supplier's instructions [W4-093], assign trained human oversight [W4-094], keep logs for at least six months [W4-095].
- Fines: up to EUR 35 000 000 or 7 % of turnover for banned practices [W4-096]; for SMEs the lower of the two applies [W4-097].

## What sources advise in practice

- Do a cost-benefit analysis and run pilots before roll-out [W4-085]; invest in data quality and governance [W4-086] (customs report with a conflict of interest, see [[ai-in-trade-and-customs]]).
- Do not use language models unsupervised where errors matter [W4-106]; see [[ai-reliability-and-hallucination]].
- SMEs cite lack of trust [W4-151] and infrastructure limits [W4-153] as barriers (qualitative pilot, small sample [W4-154]).

## Contradictions and tensions

> [!contradiction] W4-098 vs W4-093: voluntary framework vs binding duties
> Not a conflict of facts: the NIST framework is voluntary and American [W4-098]; the AI Act duties apply by law to EU users of high-risk systems [W4-093]. The NIST functions can be used to organise compliance but do not replace it.

## Gaps

- No claim says whether a used-vehicle trader's AI uses count as high-risk.
- No claim on the fine for breaching the literacy duty; only the top fine for banned practices [W4-096].
- No claim on what a small firm's risk register costs or who should own it.
