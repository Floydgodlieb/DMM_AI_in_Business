# ADR-0004: Whether every theme must be grounded in the AI Wiki

## Status

Proposed

## Context

The redesign sketches derive their authority from the AI Wiki. Every week in
every variant links to a wiki concept or synthesis page, and the document closes
with a source index of the twenty-four pages it cites. The seam along which the
module is split — strategic/organisational and economic/labour to AIBS,
engineering/practitioner to the sister module — is itself a description of how
the wiki is organised. That grounding is what makes the sketches a curriculum
proposal rather than a list of interesting topics.

The research lecturer's proposal introduces themes that have no such grounding:

- the geopolitics of AI — the Draghi report, Europe's innovation gap, state
  investment in China against big tech's influence on the American state,
  Europe's dependency on American vendors as a strategic risk;
- the model vendors' business models — closed weights behind a licensed API as a
  dependency-generating revenue model, its fragility if local models become
  competitive, open weights and state support as an alternative;
- data sovereignty, contracts and vendor lock-in as they present to an SME;
- locally hosted models as a strategic option.

These are good themes. They are also, on the evidence of the sketches' own source
index, absent from the wiki. The sketches cover the buyer's side of the
build/buy question via the firm-boundary model, but nothing on the supply side,
nothing on procurement, and nothing geopolitical.

So: is wiki coverage a precondition for a theme entering the programme, or is
the wiki one source among several?

## Options considered

**Wiki coverage is required.** Any theme without a page is out until a page
exists. Keeps the module's sourcing coherent and keeps the wiki honest — it stays
the module's actual backbone rather than a decoration. But it makes the
curriculum hostage to authoring capacity, and it excludes exactly the themes that
are most current, because the wiki lags the news by construction.

**The wiki is one source among several.** Themes are admitted on merit and cite
whatever is appropriate. Maximum freedom, immediate relevance. But the sketches'
central claim — that the split follows the wiki's structure — quietly stops being
true, and there is then no standard at all for what counts as an adequately
sourced theme.

**Wiki coverage is required for themes the lecturer teaches; student research may
go beyond it.** Under ADR-0001 these are different things. The thematic spine is
the programme's promise and should be grounded; what a team discovers inside a
theme is the research, and demanding it stay within existing pages would defeat
the purpose.

**Ungrounded themes are admitted, and producing the wiki page is part of the
work.** Students research the theme and the output is both a handbook
contribution and a new wiki page. Attractive, and it grows the wiki — but it puts
the module's own foundation in the hands of first-time authors.

## Decision

Wiki grounding is required for the fixed thematic spine — the themes the
programme commits to covering. Student research within a theme may and should go
beyond the wiki.

The four ungrounded themes above are admitted to the spine on the condition that
a wiki page exists before the module runs. Writing those four pages becomes a
prerequisite for the redesign, owned by the lecturers, not by students.

The spine is what the module promises; a promise resting on sources nobody has
appraised is not one worth making. Student research is held to a source-appraisal
standard instead — which under ADR-0001 is taught content, so the module has the
means to enforce it.

## Consequences

- Four wiki pages must be written before the redesign can run: AI geopolitics and
  the Draghi report, open versus closed weights and vendor business models, AI
  procurement and contracting for SMEs, and sovereignty and local models. This is
  a hard dependency on a deadline, not a nice-to-have.
- If those pages are not written in time, the affected themes drop out of the
  spine and the programme loses its most current material — which under ADR-0002
  is a real risk, since there are only five theme slots.
- The wiki becomes a maintenance obligation of the module rather than a
  pre-existing resource it happens to draw on.
- Geopolitical and vendor-economic material dates fast. These pages will need
  revision every year the module runs, and nobody currently owns that.
- Student research needs an explicit source-appraisal standard in the assessment
  criteria, or "beyond the wiki" becomes "whatever the search engine returned".
