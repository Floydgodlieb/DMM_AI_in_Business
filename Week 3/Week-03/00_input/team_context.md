## The case (research proposal v2)

**BAS World**, Veghel, part of BAS Group — a Dutch trader in used commercial vehicles, trailers
and machinery, 450+ staff, an AI Search tool already in production. Treated as a **frontier
case**, not a representative one: it shows what stays hard once budget and IT are no longer the
limit, and cannot describe the sector on its own.

## Who the page is for

- **Primary reader:** the owner or commercial manager of a Dutch trader in the same sector,
  10–50 staff, no IT function, selling B2B and multilingual mostly into non-EU markets. Reads one
  page to decide on one next step with AI.
- **Secondary reader:** BAS World's own commercial and operations management, who host the case.

## Research question this page serves (research proposal v2 §2)

Main question: how far can AI responsibly reach into this sector's international, multilingual
sales and customer processes, and what level of complexity can a firm here actually carry?
Week 3's trust theme maps onto sub-question **I3** (boundary: where staff draw the line on
automation that costs customer trust) and **E3** (rules and exposure: which legal duties apply).

## The team's own six quality criteria, beyond the handbook template's four floor criteria
(research proposal v2 §4 / PRD v2.1 §3, verbatim short form)

1. Names a step in the sales or documentation chain, never a category of tool.
2. Keeps time-saving automation apart from customer-touching automation, states which.
3. Names the duty that applies (AI Act, GDPR, sanctions screening), its evidence, and any
   outside supplier's hosting location.
4. Labels every figure `company-reported` or `independently-verifiable`, and `case` or `sector`.
5. Labels every recommendation by complexity: `bought tooling`, `named process owner`, or
   `in-house build` (flagged as a warning).
6. Closes with one next step: an owner, a rough cost, a three-month check.

## House style (PRD v2.1 / context.md)

Plain English, short sentences, no unexplained jargon. Every statement traces to a claim-id with
a quoted source sentence and a page/location. Every page states how much checking was done. The
handbook-template disclaimer appears on every page.
