# ADR-0005: Whether field research at companies is mandatory or optional

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Under ADR-0001 AIBS is a research module, and the research lecturer's list of
what applied research requires includes *"you must be able to do field research
to gather new data"*. Several of the proposed themes are answerable only from the
field: what knowledge is present and missing at regional SMEs, where they see
their first opportunities, which AI use cases have actually worked for them.
These are empirical questions about a population nobody has surveyed, and the
handbook's claim to be worth reading rests on answering them with evidence
rather than with a literature summary.

The proposal raises the feasibility question itself, noting a precedent: an
earlier version of the Business Intelligence module required students to
interview a company. The concern is whether every student can realistically get
access. Some have a network through an internship; others have none.

The redesign sketches contain no student fieldwork at all. They bring
practitioners in as guest speakers — the company stays the object of study, and
the students stay in the classroom.

The tension is that the module's most distinctive claim depends on the thing that
is hardest to guarantee.

## Options considered

**Mandatory, each team arranges its own access.** Strongest evidence base, and it
teaches the skill that most distinguishes applied research from desk research.
Fails whichever team cannot find a company, and that failure lands on the
students rather than on the design.

**Optional, with a desk-research alternative.** Nobody is blocked. But an
optional route that is harder than the alternative is taken by the students who
least need the practice, and the handbook ends up with a patchwork evidence base
that is difficult to consolidate under ADR-0002.

**Mandatory, with access arranged by the programme.** A pool of participating
regional SMEs recruited by the lecturers, from which teams draw. Guarantees
access and produces a comparable evidence base, because the sample is known. The
cost is recruitment work before the module starts and an ongoing relationship to
maintain — and the programme becomes answerable to those companies for how
students behave.

**Mandatory at team level, not at student level.** Each team must produce field
data; how the team divides that is theirs. A team with one well-connected member
still delivers.

## Decision

Field research is mandatory at team level, and the programme maintains a pool of
participating SMEs that teams can draw on if their own network does not yield a
contact.

Making it optional would hollow out the module's central claim. Making it
mandatory per student would fail students for lacking a network, which is not a
learning outcome. Team-level obligation with a programme-provided fallback keeps
the evidence base intact without making access a lottery, and it mirrors how
applied research actually works — access is a team asset.

## Consequences

- Recruiting and maintaining the SME pool is work that must start well before the
  module runs, and it needs a named owner. If the pool does not materialise, the
  fallback is empty and this decision silently becomes "mandatory, arrange it
  yourself".
- The programme takes on a relationship with participating companies: what they
  are promised, what they receive back, and who apologises when a team behaves
  badly.
- Interview data from named companies is personal data. Consent, anonymisation
  and retention must be settled before the first interview, and nothing
  identifying may reach the published handbook without explicit permission. The
  repository's own handling of such material is unresolved; this is not a
  hypothetical concern here.
- Team-level obligation means a team can pass while an individual member never
  conducts an interview, which weakens the individual claim to the skill and
  feeds directly into ADR-0007.
- Interview capacity limits how many teams the module can carry, and therefore
  the cohort size.
