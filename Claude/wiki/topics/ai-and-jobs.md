# AI and jobs

Exposure is high, measured effects are small so far, and people fear more than the data shows. For a firm of the reader's size the practical question is how tasks change, not how many jobs go.

## Exposure (what could change)

- One in four workers worldwide are in an occupation with some exposure to generative AI; most jobs will be transformed rather than made redundant [W4-122].
- Almost 40 percent of global employment is exposed to AI [W4-183], about 60 percent of jobs in advanced economies [W4-184]; about half of those may be negatively affected, the rest could gain productivity [W4-185]. Exposure is not jobs lost [W4-184].

## What has been measured (small so far)

- Euro area: little evidence yet of a substantial effect of AI on employment [W4-112].
- Denmark (NBER working paper): precise null effects on earnings and hours, ruling out effects above 2% [W4-129].
- US firms (NBER working paper): strong task substitution, but modest overall employment effects as adopting firms grow [W4-136]; the impact on the make-up of the workforce is limited [W4-138].
- SMEs in seven countries (OECD survey, self-reported): 83% say generative AI has had no effect on overall staff need [W4-175].
- Income: the NBER macro paper predicts AI will widen the gap between capital and labor income [W4-109].

## What people think

- In the Netherlands, 43 percent of people in paid work use AI at work [W4-139]; 75 percent expect certain jobs to disappear and 64 percent expect staff to lose knowledge and skills [W4-140]; 57 percent expect higher productivity [W4-141].

## Contradictions and tensions

> [!contradiction] W4-184 vs W4-112: about 60 percent of jobs exposed vs little employment effect seen
> Exposure is a potential, estimated from task content [W4-183]; the euro area and Danish data measure outcomes two years in [W4-112] [W4-129]. Not a conflict of facts, but the headline exposure figure must not be read as expected job losses.

> [!contradiction] W4-140 vs W4-175: Dutch adults expect job losses; SMEs report no effect on staffing
> Opinion of adults in general [W4-140] against SMEs' own reports of what has happened [W4-175]. Different populations and different questions (expectation versus experience).

> [!contradiction] W4-185 vs W4-136: about half of exposed jobs negatively affected vs modest net employment effect
> The IMF measure is potential complementarity [W4-185]; the US firm study finds losses in exposed occupations offset by growth at adopting firms [W4-136].

## Gaps

- No claim on jobs in trade, sales administration or translation specifically.
- No claim on the effect for very small firms beyond the OECD self-reports.
