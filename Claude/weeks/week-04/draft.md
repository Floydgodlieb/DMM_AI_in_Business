## Why this question matters for a trader like you

Most firms that look at AI and then leave it alone give the same reason: they don't know enough about it.

- Across the EU, firms that considered AI but did not use it most often said they lacked the expertise (70.89%) [W4-022, independently-verifiable, sector]. Unclear legal consequences (52.52%) and privacy worries (48.83%) came next [W4-022, independently-verifiable, sector].
- Dutch micro-businesses say the same: 71.6% of those that decided against AI in 2025 said they lacked experience [W4-023, independently-verifiable, sector]. They are smaller than your firm, so treat it as a rough signal for your firm [W4-023, independently-verifiable, sector].
- In trade and logistics, almost two thirds of firms name a lack of knowledge and skills [W4-014, company-reported, sector]. Only seven percent have a concrete AI policy [W4-013, company-reported, sector].
- Owners know what they are missing. They ask for practical knowledge, information, guidance and financial support [W4-029, independently-verifiable, sector].

Research points the same way. A European study finds that small and medium-sized firms lag behind on technologies such as AI because they are complex and costly [W4-055, independently-verifiable, sector]. Its data is from before generative AI [W4-055, independently-verifiable, sector]. A review of the research reports that limited AI education holds small firms back [W4-063, independently-verifiable, sector]. The same review reports that practical training gives staff confidence and makes AI easier to use [W4-066, independently-verifiable, sector].

**What does that mean for a firm of 10–50 people?** Firms your size use AI the least. In 2024, 17.8% of Dutch firms with 10–19 staff used AI, against 59.2% of firms with 500 or more [W2-002, independently-verifiable, sector]. Most Dutch firms with 10 or more staff had no IT specialist on the payroll [W4-025, independently-verifiable, sector].

- The frontier case (BAS World — not the sector average): even this much larger firm made a point of keeping the knowledge in-house. It had its AI agents built so it could keep developing them itself, without depending on its supplier [W2-023, company-reported, case].

## What you depend on, and why it can change

Without an IT department, you rely on outside help. Here is what exists, and the limits of each option.

- **Outside help, and your own knowledge.** Small firms often team up with outside organisations because their own resources are limited [W4-067, independently-verifiable, sector]. Yet a large EU study found that a firm's own capabilities matter more for adopting AI than outside support [W4-048, independently-verifiable, sector]. That survey ran from February to May 2020, before generative AI [W4-050, independently-verifiable, sector].
- **Digiwerkplaats mkb (Avans).** It helps business owners with a digitalisation question in West-Brabant and Noordoost-Brabant [W4-017, company-reported, sector].
- **EU digital innovation hubs (EDIHs).** EDIH Zuid-Nederland helps small manufacturing firms in Zeeland, Noord-Brabant and Limburg with digitalisation [W4-018, company-reported, sector]. It is meant for manufacturing, so a trader may not qualify [W4-019, company-reported, sector]. Across the EU, the hubs report that they helped 90% of their users improve their digital maturity [W4-034, company-reported, sector]. That figure comes from the hubs' own monitoring [W4-035, company-reported, sector].
- **KVK.** It warns that personal data or business information you type into an AI tool may not be protected [W4-020, independently-verifiable, sector]. It also warns that text written by an AI tool can contain mistakes [W4-021, independently-verifiable, sector].
- **Your AI supplier.** The government advises asking your supplier what training and support you will need [W4-010, independently-verifiable, sector].
- **What could change.** The AI Act obliges the EU and its member states to help small firms meet the rules [W4-004, independently-verifiable, sector]. The SER, the government's social and economic advisory council, advises practical support for small firms [W4-030, independently-verifiable, sector]. It also wants the government to make sure firms can share knowledge in a network [W4-031, independently-verifiable, sector].

## The duties that apply

- **AI Act:** If your firm uses AI systems, you must make sure the staff who use them know enough about AI [W4-003, independently-verifiable, sector]. This has applied since 2 February 2025, and supervision and enforcement start on 3 August 2026 [W4-006, independently-verifiable, sector]. You do not have to test your staff's knowledge [W4-005, independently-verifiable, sector]. The government advises building awareness and giving enough training on the risks and opportunities of AI [W4-009, independently-verifiable, sector].
- **GDPR (incl. who processes your data, and where):** Personal data you type into an AI tool may not be protected [W4-020, independently-verifiable, sector]. If a supplier processes personal data for you, the European Commission has standard contract clauses for that under Article 28(7) GDPR [W2-020, independently-verifiable, sector]. Ask each supplier where it stores your data before you sign.
- **Sanctions screening / Regulation 833/2014 (export documents):** Some goods may not be exported to some countries, or only with a permit [W2-005, independently-verifiable, sector]. For Russia, the legal basis for the export bans is Council Regulation (EU) No 833/2014 [W2-008, independently-verifiable, sector].
