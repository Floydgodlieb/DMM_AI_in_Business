Week 1 - Research Proposal - BAS World

Week 1 – 7 September 2026

Research Proposal (First Draft)

AI in international, multilingual sales and customer processes in the used commercial vehicle trading sector — BAS World case

1. Target Audience

Note upfront: BAS World (Veghel) has 450+ employees, 60+ years of experience, and trades worldwide in 150+ countries — this is not a classic SME. To keep the case specific and manageable, this research focuses on one well-defined part of the sector: the international sales and customer service process, from first contact to document handling.

Sector, size, region: international sales and customer service teams at large, multilingual trading platforms for used commercial vehicles and machinery, typically operating from a central showroom/hub location in the Netherlands.

Named partner: BAS World (Veghel), which handles more than 15,000 transactions per year in 18+ languages and serves customers in 150+ countries.

2. What Does the Target Audience Need?

Draghi's diagnosis is aimed primarily at SMEs, but his core point — that European companies must embrace AI without becoming dependent on non-European technology vendors, and without sacrificing trust and quality for speed — is at least as relevant for a large international sector player. Because platforms such as BAS World are already further along with AI than the average SME (BAS World already runs 'AI Search' in production), the question becomes sharper: where is the line between useful automation and losing the personal service that distinguishes the sector?

They mainly need to know: (1) where AI is already used across the sector's sales and service processes, and how that adoption came about; (2) where in the broader sales and documentation process room remains for expansion; and (3) which risks — data privacy, cross-border regulation, dependency on foreign AI vendors — apply.

3. Explanation of the Research Problem

At organisations that, unlike the average SME, already have a first AI application in production, the question shifts from 'should we use AI?' to 'where can and should we expand it?'. It is currently unclear how consistently and broadly AI is already used across this sector, which parts of the process outside tools like AI Search still run entirely manually, and which of those parts are suited to further AI support without undermining the personal service that customer reviews across the sector consistently cite as a reason for satisfaction. This research should close that gap: a concrete picture of current sector-wide use, alongside a well-founded assessment of where AI could be used further — grounded in one detailed case, BAS World.

4. Research Question and Sub-Questions

Main question: How are international, multilingual trading platforms in the used commercial vehicle and machinery sector currently using AI, and where else could AI be used?

Internal Analysis

  • SQ1: How is AI currently used within the sales and customer service process at platforms in this sector (for example, BAS World's 'AI Search'), and which roles within the organisation are typically involved?

  • SQ2: Where in the broader process — from lead to transaction, transport, and documentation — do employees in this sector see room for (additional) AI use?

External Analysis

  • SQ3: What AI applications are used by comparable international platforms for used vehicles and machinery, and how do these compare to tools already in use, such as BAS World's 'AI Search'?

  • SQ4: What risks around data privacy, cross-border regulation (GDPR, export control, customs), and dependency on foreign AI vendors apply to international trading platforms in this sector?

5. Methodology

Desk research: a literature review of AI applications at comparable international platforms for used vehicles and machinery (for example Machineryline, TruckScout24), of GDPR and export regulations for international trade, and the Draghi analysis for the broader European context. This primarily serves SQ3 and SQ4 (external analysis).

Field research: one company visit to BAS World in weeks 2–4, preferably with someone from the international sales or customer service team, focused on current AI use and where employees themselves see opportunities. This primarily serves SQ1 and SQ2 (internal analysis), providing a concrete, sector-representative case.

It is therefore clear in advance which sub-questions each method serves: desk research answers the external sub-questions (market and risks), while the company visit answers the internal sub-questions (current use and perceived opportunities), using BAS World as the sector's case example.

6. Quality Criteria

  • Concretely applicable to international, multilingual sales and documentation processes across the sector, not generic 'AI for SMEs'.

  • Explicitly distinguishes between automation that saves time and automation that undermines personal contact and trust.

  • Names risks around data privacy, regulation, and vendor dependency alongside opportunities, in line with the Draghi discussion.

  • Explicitly acknowledges that the case example is a larger, more advanced player than a classic SME, and draws lessons that are also relevant to smaller players in the same sector.

  • Concludes with a concrete, actionable recommendation on the next AI step in the customer process.
