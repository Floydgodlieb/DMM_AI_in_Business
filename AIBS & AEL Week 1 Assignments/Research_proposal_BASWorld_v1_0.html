1. The research problem

What Draghi found

The report on European competitiveness of 9 September 2024 traces Europe's growth gap with the United States and China to technology, though not to any shortage of ideas. The problem is diffusion. European firms keep pouring research money into mature sectors while digital tools spread slowly through the businesses that already exist. Draghi lists the barriers plainly: a fragmented single market, regulatory load (55 percent of EU SMEs name administrative and regulatory burden as their biggest obstacle), thin access to capital, missing skills, too little scale. He treats reliance on high-risk suppliers outside Europe as a separate worry rather than the main one.

Why this lands on BAS World

Diffusion is the open question at BAS World. One AI feature runs in production, the AI Search on basworld.com, and multilingual first contact is handled by a chat and flowbot layer. Neither tells us how far AI reaches into the rest of the sales and documentation chain.

CBS figures sharpen the picture. In 2025, 17 percent of Dutch firms used AI, rising to 66 percent among firms with 250 staff or more, while wholesale and trade sat at 12 percent and transport and storage at 7 percent. BAS World lands in a size class where AI is already common, inside a sector where it stays rare. Whether it is ahead of its trade, or only ahead at the shop window, is something we have to find out instead of assuming.

What the company needs to work out

Which parts of the sales and service chain already run on AI, and who owns them. Which steps between a first lead and a delivered export document still run entirely on people. Which of those can take support without damaging the personal contact this trade competes on. And which duties shape the answer: EU AI Act transparency rules now in force, GDPR on customer and interview data, and the screening of buyers and final destinations.

"Our search is already AI. Where is the next one, and how do I know it will not cost me the customer relationship or land me a compliance problem?"

2. Research question and sub-questions

Main question. Where and how does BAS World use AI in its international, multilingual sales and customer process, and where can that use responsibly grow?

#AngleSub-question
1External: marketWhich AI applications are visible at comparable international platforms for used vehicles and machinery (Ritchie Bros with its valuation and price-results tooling, Mascus, TruckScout24, Machineryline), which process step does each one sit in, and what does that suggest is workable at BAS World's scale?
2External: riskWhich rules and exposures apply to AI in customer-facing cross-border trade in used vehicles: AI Act transparency duties under Article 50 (applicable from 2 August 2026) and AI literacy under Article 4, GDPR on customer and interview data, plus sanctions exposure and third country circumvention risk in used transport vehicles?
3Internal: current stateIn which steps of BAS World's own chain (lead, quotation, transaction, transport, export documentation, after-sales) does AI or rule-based automation run today, which of those were bought in and which built in-house, and which roles own them?
4Internal: the limitWhich steps in that same chain do BAS World's sales, service and documentation staff find most repetitive or most tied to language, and where do they themselves draw the line past which automation would cost customer trust?

How the four fit together. Questions 1 and 2 supply the outside benchmark and the binding constraints. Questions 3 and 4 supply the real starting point inside the company, plus the limit its own people accept. Drop question 3 and we recommend something that already exists. Drop question 4 and we recommend something nobody will use.

4. Quality criteria for the handbook

Floor criteria from the chapter template. Clear to a non-technical reader. Accurate against appraised sources. Covers responsible AI. Useful to an owner.

Ours on top of those

#Criterion
1Every recommendation attaches to a named step in the sales or documentation chain, never to a category of tool.
2Time-saving automation and automation that touches the customer relationship stay apart, and each recommendation says which of the two it is.
3Each recommendation names the duty that applies (AI Act, GDPR, sanctions screening) and what evidence would show compliance.
4Every figure carries a label, company-reported or independently verifiable, and the first is never dressed up as the second.
5Each recommendation states what a trader with 10 to 50 staff in the same business would need to run a smaller version of it. This is how we honour the handbook's SME framing while working with a large partner.
6The page closes with one concrete next step: an owner, a rough cost, and a way to tell inside three months whether it worked.

Cross-check. This list has to match "Output qualities" in the PRD. It currently does.

5. Methodology

Desk research

ElementHow we handle it
SourcesEU primary material (consolidated AI Act text, Regulation 833/2014 with its amending packages, OLAF publications), Dutch statistical and registry material (CBS, KvK), the Draghi report, sector platforms, trade press.
Selection and appraisalEach source gets scored on credibility (who publishes it, what interest they hold), relevance (which sub-question it feeds), and recency (regulation dated to the version in force in September 2026, not to the package that first introduced it). A company's own description of itself counts as a claim about the company, never as independent evidence. Where sources disagree we report the disagreement instead of picking the bigger number: BAS World states more than 18 languages, while the Trengo customer case describing its chat layer specifies 13 localised language journeys. Those cannot be the same measure.
LanguageDutch sources get appraised in Dutch, before any translation, and are translated only for drafting. PRD section 5 covers the mechanics.
FeedsSub-questions 1 and 2.

Field research: one company visit, weeks 2 to 4

ElementHow we handle it
WhoOne or two people from international sales or customer service, and where we can get it, one from export documentation, since that is where sanctions and customs work lands.
SubjectA walk through the chain from lead to delivered document, with questions per step on tooling, time spent, language and irritation, followed by the boundary question from sub-question 4.
Recording and handlingRecorded only with consent given beforehand. The transcript stays inside the team. People are anonymised by role rather than by name. The recording goes to no service outside the environment the team has agreed on. Everything is deleted once the module ends.
LanguageThe visit will most likely run in Dutch. The recording goes through the platform's appraise-then-translate path (PRD section 5) so that language shuts no team member out of the material.
