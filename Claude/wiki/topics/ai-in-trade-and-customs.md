# AI in trade, translation and customs

The library has evidence that machine translation and generative AI help cross-border trade, but from consumer marketplaces, not business-to-business trade in vehicles or machinery. On customs it has the EU reform and a report on customs authorities' own AI use.

## Translation and language

- Machine translation on eBay raised exports by 17.5% (NBER working paper) [W4-071]; the authors read it as causal evidence that language barriers hinder trade [W4-072]. It is a consumer and small-seller marketplace [W4-071] and the paper dates from August 2018, before generative AI [W4-074].
- On a cross-border e-commerce platform, generative AI was deployed in seven workflows including live chat translation [W4-090]; sales rose in most workflows [W4-087]. This is a preprint, not peer-reviewed, with authors tied to the partner company [W4-087].

## Trade at large (WTO)

- Of firms already using AI, nearly 90 per cent report tangible benefits in trade-related activities and 56 per cent say it improved trade-risk management (self-reported) [W4-075].
- The WTO lists better supply-chain visibility, automated customs clearance, fewer language barriers, market intelligence, contract enforcement and help for small firms with complex rules [W4-076].
- Scenarios suggest global trade up by 34 to 37 per cent by 2040 [W4-077].

## Customs and the export-document step

- The EU adopted a major customs reform on 16 September 2026 [W4-079]; a new EU Customs Authority will run a single EU Customs Data Hub [W4-081]. Using it becomes mandatory for e-commerce on 1 July 2028 and for all traders from 1 March 2034 [W4-080]. Trust and Check traders may self-release goods [W4-082]. See [[sanctions-and-export-screening]].
- A World Customs Organization report says customs administrations use AI to automate routine processes, assess risk, detect fraud and speed up clearance [W4-084]. It was funded by China Customs and has not been through formal WCO approval [W4-083], so it is a weak source.
- The same report recommends a cost-benefit analysis and pilots before full roll-out [W4-085], and says data quality and governance are fundamental [W4-086].

## Contradictions and tensions

> [!contradiction] W4-075 vs W4-087: self-reported benefits in almost all users vs measured effects from no impact to 16.3%
> The WTO figure is firms reporting on themselves [W4-075]; the field experiment measures sales, and finds no detectable impact in some workflows [W4-087]. Self-reports run higher than measured effects.

## Gaps

- No claim on AI for business-to-business used-vehicle or machinery trade, or on translation of technical specifications.
- Nothing says whether AI will touch the customs declaration the trader files; the reform claims do not mention AI.
- No claim ties the data hub deadlines to a Dutch trader's current export documents.
