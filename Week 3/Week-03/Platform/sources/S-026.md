excerpt: |
  (decision date) 14 February 2024

  (what happened) Air Canada's website chatbot told a customer he could apply for a
  bereavement fare refund retroactively; Air Canada's human staff later said this was wrong,
  and the airline argued it could not be held liable for what its own chatbot said.

  (finding) the tribunal found Air Canada owed a duty of care to chatbot users and "did not
  take reasonable care to ensure its chatbot was accurate," amounting to negligent
  misrepresentation; the customer was awarded CAD 650.88.

  note: outside the AI Act's 2024-or-later recency preference, kept as a foundational, still-
  widely-cited precedent (same treatment context.md and week 2's appraised.md gave the 2024
  Draghi report) — it is the clearest primary record of the general principle this page needs:
  a business is answerable for what its customer-facing AI tells a customer, on ordinary
  negligence and agency-law grounds, independent of whether any AI-specific regulation applies
  to that tool at all.
