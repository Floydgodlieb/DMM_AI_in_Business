# Overview

A Dutch trader of the reader's size is less likely than average to use AI [W2-003], mostly because it lacks the knowledge to start [W4-022]; the law requires it to build that knowledge, and the evidence says it must be built inside the firm, with outside help as a start. A new scientific layer shows real gains for less-experienced staff, errors outside the tool's competence, and small measured labour-market effects so far.

## The picture, weeks 2 and 4 together

1. **Adoption is low for small firms.** About one in five Dutch firms with 10+ staff used AI in 2024 [W2-001], and the smallest of those used it least [W2-002]. EU, OECD, G7 and ECB sources all find small firms behind large ones [W4-116] [W4-143] [W4-179]. → [[ai-adoption-rates]]
2. **The barrier is knowledge.** Lack of expertise is the first reason firms give for not using AI [W4-022], and most firms of this size have no ICT specialist [W4-025]. → [[knowledge-gap]]
3. **The law makes knowledge a duty.** Any firm using AI must build AI literacy among its people [W4-003], enforced from August 2026 [W4-006]; heavier duties apply only to high-risk systems [W4-093]. → [[ai-literacy-duty]], [[ai-act]], [[ai-risk-management]]
4. **Outside help exists but is no shortcut.** SMEs ask for practical guidance [W4-029]; the nearby hubs target other sectors [W4-019], and bought-in advice fades [W4-042]. → [[external-knowledge-routes]]
5. **Capability is built inside.** Own capabilities matter more than outside support [W4-048], and management has to lead it [W4-038]. → [[learning-inside-the-firm]]
6. **What you depend on can change.** Hosting can shift by default [W2-025] [W2-028], and Europe depends on foreign AI providers [W2-019]. → [[vendor-dependency]], [[data-location-and-gdpr]], [[geopolitics-of-ai]]
7. **Exports carry a screening duty.** Regulation 833/2014 bans certain exports to Russia [W2-008]; the EU customs data hub becomes mandatory for all traders from 2034 [W4-080]. → [[sanctions-and-export-screening]], [[ai-in-trade-and-customs]]
8. **Scientific evidence layer.** In field experiments, gains are largest for novice workers [W4-126] [W4-169]; outside the tool's frontier, correctness fell [W4-165]; language models invent facts [W4-101]. → [[genai-productivity-at-work]], [[ai-reliability-and-hallucination]]
9. **Jobs and the economy: small effects so far.** The euro area shows little employment effect [W4-112], Danish data show null effects on earnings [W4-129], and a macro estimate is modest [W4-107]. → [[ai-and-jobs]], [[ai-and-the-economy]]

The frontier case, [[bas-world]], shows one answer to point 6 at scale: a model-agnostic setup [W2-022]. It is a case, not the sector average.

## Main tensions

- Dutch, EU, ECB and OECD adoption figures measure different things ([[ai-adoption-rates]]).
- Large task-level gains in single-firm experiments against a modest macro estimate and null earnings effects ([[genai-productivity-at-work]]).
- High job exposure against little measured employment effect ([[ai-and-jobs]]).
- Hubs report success by their own monitoring; an independent trial of bought-in advice finds the gain fades ([[external-knowledge-routes]]).
- Most quantified evidence is a working paper, a preprint or from before generative AI, and none is from business-to-business trade in vehicles or machinery ([[nber]], [[flash-eurobarometer-2020]]).
