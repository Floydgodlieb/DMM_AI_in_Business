AI Product Requirements Document (First Draft)

Platform for handbook research and production — BAS World case

1. About

This product is an agentic research and publishing platform that lets the project team publish one new handbook page per week on AI at BAS World, an international, multilingual trading platform for used commercial vehicles and machinery (Veghel, NL). The team must deliver a well-founded page every week, drawing on both English and Dutch-language sources, without losing human control over quality.

2. Market Insights

General AI research assistants (e.g. Perplexity, Notion AI) are widely available, but none are tailored to this project's specific combination: a fixed weekly publishing cadence, a fixed chapter template, and mandatory processing of Dutch-language sources alongside English ones.

  • Customer segments: the research team (4 students, rotating build roles: product manager, developer, tester, deployer); downstream, BAS World's management, who read the published pages.

  • User personas: a team researcher without a deep AI background, who must turn raw sources into a publishable page within a week; an SME reader (a BAS World manager) without a technical AI background, reading the page to decide on the next AI step.

  • Technology: the platform relies on an agentic CLI (directed by the team, not hand-coded), existing language/translation models, and the course's existing wiki/site infrastructure.

3. The Problem

  • Use cases: find and assess Dutch and English sources for relevance/reliability per sub-question; translate Dutch sources accurately into English; summarise sources into a page that follows the handbook template; have the team review and publish it.

  • Pain point: manual searching, translating, assessing and writing takes too much time to sustain weekly for six weeks alongside the rest of the programme. Use of Dutch sources also currently depends on which team member happens to speak Dutch.

Problem statement: the research team spends too much time manually searching, translating and assessing Dutch and English sources, and therefore does not always deliver a publication-ready, well-founded handbook page every week. AI is uniquely suited here, as language detection, translation, summarisation and source assessment are tasks a language model performs faster and more consistently than a small team can within the available time.

Hypothesis: automating source collection, translation and page-concept generation — with the team acting as reviewer and editor rather than executor — makes the process faster and more consistent, and reduces dependence on which team member happens to speak Dutch.

4. The Solution

  • Ideation: agentic source finder (NL/EN); automatic language detection and NL→EN translation with verification; source reliability/relevance scoring; per-sub-question summarisation; page-concept generator following the handbook template; review checklist tool.

Leveraging AI: translation, summarisation and source assessment are language- and time-bound tasks a language model performs structurally faster and more consistently than a four-person team can within a week, without removing human control over final quality.

AI MVP: no custom model is trained. The MVP is a hybrid solution — existing language models and translation services are directed via an agentic CLI, with prompt-driven tasks for language detection, translation, summarisation, source assessment and page generation.

Roadmap: based on RICE scores — review checklist tool first (highest score, lowest effort), then the translation pipeline, then source scoring, then the page-concept generator, which builds on the first three.

Technical architecture: the team (client) directs tasks via an agentic CLI; the agent retrieves sources (web/files), sends text to language-model and translation APIs, and writes results back to the course's existing wiki/site. No new infrastructure is built.

Assumptions & constraints: agentic CLI rather than hand-coded; reuse of existing wiki/site infrastructure; must be runnable by a non-coding team member; every automated step has a manual fallback; assumes BAS World is willing to host at least one visit in weeks 2–4.

Open question: BAS World's scale (450+ employees, global reach) is well beyond a typical SME. Does the research stay scoped to the sales/customer process, or is a smaller SME in the same sector added, in consultation with the lecturers?

  • Risks: translation errors in figures/quotes → mandatory verification against the original; misplaced trust in unreliable sources → explicit, traceable reliability scoring; dependency on external AI/translation services → manual fallback at every step; sensitivity of interview material → keep the transcript internal, no automatic publication without review.

5. Requirements

User journey: source input/retrieval → language detection & translation → source assessment → per-sub-question summarisation → page-concept generation → human review & editing → publication to the wiki/site.

  • Functional: retrieve or accept sources via agentic search; detect language and translate Dutch sources, keeping the original; score source reliability and relevance with a traceable rationale; summarise and structure material per sub-question; generate a page concept from the handbook template; publish the reviewed page to the existing wiki/site.

  • Non-functional: usable by a team member without a coding background; every automated step has a manual fallback; turnaround per page fits within one week; no storage of sensitive interview material outside the team's own environment.

  • AI & data: public Dutch and English sources plus one interview transcript; no custom model training, existing language-model and translation APIs only; temporary storage of source text and translations during processing.

6. Challenges

Sufficient data is available: public sources plus at least one interview. No extra budget is required, as existing language-model and translation services fall within the course's resources. The main uncertainty is user conviction — whether the team trusts an automated translation and assessment step enough to build on it, or whether much still ends up being checked manually in practice.

8. Measuring Success

  • Metrics: number of pages published on time per week (target: 1 per week, for six weeks); time from raw source material to a publication-ready concept.

  • AI-specific metrics: translation accuracy (share of checked translations with no deviation in figures, amounts or quotes); share of page claims traceable to a source.

North Star metric: number of publication-ready handbook pages per week that pass human review without major manual rewriting.

9. Launching

  • Stakeholders & communication: the team itself, the AIBS/AEL lecturers and assessors, and a BAS World contact for the interview; kept informed via the weekly publication and the portfolio.

  • Rollout strategy: phased rollout per the roadmap in Section 4 — review checklist tool first, then the translation pipeline, then source assessment, then the page generator — so the team can already publish each week while the platform keeps growing.
